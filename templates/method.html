How the checks work

Both checks follow the way an auditor works: find the passage that talks about a rule, judge that passage, and keep it as evidence.

The three results

CompliantThe documents follow the rule.
Non-compliantThe documents talk about the rule but break it.
Not addressedNothing in the documents covers the rule.

Policy check

  1. Read the policy. Every sentence with a requirement word (must, shall, prohibited…) becomes a rule. Its wording decides its type: a minimum (at least 12 characters), a maximum (within 24 hours, every 6 months), a prohibition (must not) or a plain obligation.
  2. Split the documents into sentences.
  3. Find the best sentence for each rule. The score is the average of two similarities: word overlap (TF-IDF on character n-grams, so report matches reported) and meaning (Sentence-BERT embeddings, so revoked matches disabled). If the best score is below 0.22 the rule is not addressed.
  4. Judge the sentence. For number rules the quantity is extracted, converted to a common unit and compared (once a year = 365 days, more than the 180 allowed). For obligations, weak wording such as optional or encouraged means non-compliant. For prohibitions the sentence must contain a restriction.
  5. Explain. The sentence, its document and the reason are shown with every result. A match scoring below 0.40 is marked review and is never accepted as proof of compliance: a possible violation is still reported, but a weak "compliant" becomes not addressed.

On the sample company (19 rules, 5 documents, answers labelled by us) the check gives the right result for 18 rules. The set is small and was also used to choose the two thresholds, so this is a demonstration, not a benchmark.

Contract check

This check is learned from data: ContractNLI, 607 real non-disclosure agreements in which lawyers labelled 17 rules per contract and marked the sentences that prove each label. We trained on 423 contracts and tested on 123 that the model never saw.

  1. Evidence finder. One Logistic Regression per rule scores every sentence (TF-IDF features) and keeps the top 3.
  2. Compliance judge. A second Logistic Regression per rule reads those 3 sentences and predicts one of the three results.

Limits

  • The policy check compares one sentence with one rule. It can pick a sentence on a related topic that does not really state the requirement; such cases usually carry a low score and are marked review.
  • The contract model is trained on NDAs only and is weakest on the rare non-compliant result.
  • Scanned PDFs (images) are not read. Only English is supported.
